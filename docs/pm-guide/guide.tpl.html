Running a pool in the D2 Build Scheduler

What this is

The Build Scheduler holds the schedule and the completion record for every D2 pool.

A pool is broken into 46 sub-phases, from the sales handoff through to the 30-day follow-up. Each one has a crew, a set of dates and a checklist of tasks. Each completed sub-phase records who signed it off and when.

The same information reaches people four ways:

The siteEvery pool's schedule, and the views for reading it by day, by week, by crew or by pool.
The calendarWhich pool is being worked on when, in Outlook and Teams.
The morning emailWhat is scheduled today, and how much recently finished work has been signed off.
The phone appToday's tasks, ticked off and signed on site.

Shared calendar

The pool calendar

Every live pool, on one calendar, in Outlook and Teams.

To open it: in the site's left-hand menu click Calendar. It can also be reached in Outlook, under D2 Build Scheduler in the list of group calendars on the left.

Clicking a block shows the crew, how many tasks are done, whether it has been signed off, and a link straight back to the sub-phase.

What appearsEvery sub-phase of every Active pool. A pool becomes Active when the PM commits it, which is covered under The life of a pool below. Planned and On Hold pools are not shown.
Marked freeBlocks are set to "free", so they never make anyone look busy or block a meeting invitation.
UpdatesOvernight. A change made this afternoon appears on the calendar in the morning.
Edits made here do not stick

The calendar is rebuilt from the schedule each night, so anything changed directly on a calendar entry is overwritten. Change the sub-phase in the Build Scheduler and the calendar follows.

Why the weekly can show a different figure

The weekly runs on Friday afternoon, so it scores sign-off compliance on work that has already finished. Sub-phases finishing late Friday or on Saturday land in the following Monday's daily summary instead. Neither figure is wrong; they cover different windows.

The shape of it

The life of a pool

Every pool moves through the same six stages.

  • Requested A pool is asked for

    The PM fills in one short form: pool name, job number, address, start date and project manager.

    Takes a minute.
  • Built The system writes the schedule

    46 sub-phases in the right order, each with dates that skip Sundays and holidays, a default crew, and a task checklist. Roughly 295 tasks in all.

    Happens on its own. Allow about ten minutes.
  • Planned The pool exists, but nothing is happening

    No crew is dispatched, nothing appears on the calendar, and it stays out of the summaries. This is the window for correcting the durations, which all start at a placeholder of two days, and for adjusting the sequence.

    The PM's real work on a new pool happens here.
  • Active The pool goes live

    Changing one field from Planned to Active turns everything on: crews are dispatched each morning, the pool appears on the shared calendar, and it counts in the daily and weekly summaries.

    One deliberate step, so nothing goes live by accident.
  • Running Work happens and gets recorded

    Tasks are ticked off and each sub-phase is signed off as it finishes. When something slips, one reschedule moves the rest of the pool around it.

    Weeks or months, depending on the build.
  • Closed out Every stage is signed

    The pool finishes with a complete record: what was done, when, and by whose name.

    Nothing to archive. It simply stops appearing in this week's work.
The one that catches people

A pool sits at Planned until somebody moves it to Active. That step is easy to forget, and a pool nobody has made live will sit there quietly doing nothing at all.

Conflicts

Is a crew booked in two places at once?

Each night the system checks whether any crew is scheduled on two pools on the same day, and writes what it finds onto the affected sub-phases. The Conflicts view lists them.

Subcontractors are deliberately left out of this check, because they can put more than one team out on the same day. Only D2's own crews are checked.

Quick start

Starting a new pool

Fill in one short form. The system builds the 46 sub-phases and roughly 295 tasks behind it.

Building a pool takes several minutes

Saving the form does not create the pool straight away. Behind the scenes the system writes 46 sub-phases and around 295 tasks, one at a time, and that normally runs for about nine to ten minutes. The request sits at Building the whole time. Nothing has gone wrong and the form does not need saving again. Leave it, refresh the page after a few minutes, and wait for Done.

  1. Open Pools › Start a New Pool from the tabs at the top of the page.
  2. Click the green + New button at the top left. A panel slides in from the right.
  3. Fill in every box: Title (the pool name, such as Reyes Residence), JobNumber, Address, City, StartDate and Project Manager. Leave Assign Default Crews ticked.
  4. Click Save at the bottom of the panel.
  5. A new row appears with a Status of Requested.
  6. The status moves to Building. Leave it alone for several minutes.
  7. Refresh the page. Once the status reads Done the pool exists, and an email confirms what was built.
What gets built

A complete pool: 46 sub-phases in the right order, each with dates that skip Sundays and holidays, a default crew, and its own task checklist.

The pool is not finished yet

Two things still have to happen, and the pool does nothing at all until the second one is done. Both are covered next: put in the real durations, then make it live.

Quick start

Putting in the real durations

Every sub-phase is created at two working days, because the system has no way of knowing any better.

A freshly built pool has the right 46 stages in the right order, and every one of them is two days long. Replacing those with real durations is what turns it into a schedule.

To change one sub-phase:

  1. Open Lists › All Assignments from the tabs at the top of the page.
  2. Find the sub-phase and click its name. A panel opens showing its details.
  3. Click Edit all at the top left of the panel.
  4. Click in the DurationDays box and type the number of working days the stage really takes.
  5. Click Save at the bottom.
  6. Repeat for each sub-phase that is wrong.
Then reflow the pool

Changing a duration does not move anything on its own. It only records how long that stage should take. Once the durations are right, raise a Reschedule Pool request starting from sub-phase 1, and the whole pool is recalculated around the new lengths. Rescheduling is covered below.

Quick start

Making a pool live

This is the step most often forgotten, and nothing happens without it.

A brand-new pool has a status of Planned. While it is Planned, no crew is dispatched for it, it does not appear on the shared calendar, and it is left out of the daily and weekly summaries.

That is deliberate. It gives time to fix the durations and adjust the sequence before anyone acts on a plan that is still being written.

To make a pool live:

  1. Open Pools from the tabs at the top of the page.
  2. Find the pool. The Status column shows Planned.
  3. Click the pool's name. A panel opens showing its details.
  4. Click Edit all at the top left of the panel.
  5. Click the Status box and choose Active from the list.
  6. Click Save at the bottom.

From the next morning the pool behaves like any other live job: crews are dispatched, it appears on the calendar, and it counts in the summaries.

Also useful

On Hold works the same way in reverse, and is set through exactly the same six steps. A job that stops for a while can be put On Hold rather than deleted. It stops dispatching and drops off the calendar, and everything is still there when it restarts.

Quick start

Moving a pool when something slips

Rain, a late inspection, materials that did not arrive. The schedule does not have to be redone by hand.

  1. Open Pools › Reschedule a Pool from the tabs at the top of the page.
  2. Click the green + New button at the top left.
  3. In Title, type something that will still make sense in a month, such as Whitfield, reflow after weather delay.
  4. In JobNumber, type the pool's job number, such as D2-1043.
  5. In FromSequence, type the number of the sub-phase to start from. Everything from there onwards moves. Everything before it stays put. To reflow a whole pool, type 1.
  6. NewStartDate is optional. Fill it in to move that sub-phase to a particular date. Leave it empty to keep the same start and simply recalculate using the durations.
  7. Click Save at the bottom of the panel.
  8. The row shows Running, then Done. Refresh the page to see it change. An email reports how many sub-phases moved and where the pool now finishes.

The reflow recalculates every following sub-phase in working days, skipping Sundays and holidays, so there is no need to count them.

Pin anything that must not move

A booked inspection or a confirmed delivery should not slide just because the work before it slipped.

To pin one: open the sub-phase from All Assignments, click Edit all, tick the Locked box, and click Save. The reflow then schedules around it instead of moving it.

Anything already signed off is left alone as well, whether it is locked or not.

In the field

The sign-off app

A phone app where the day's work is ticked off and signed.

To open it: on the home page, click Open the D2 Sign-off app near the top. On a phone, the same link can be saved to the home screen.

The app opens on the sub-phases scheduled for today. Tapping one opens its task list. Tapping a task ticks it off, and the sub-phase is signed off once the work is finished.

Three rules shape how it works:

Nothing half-signedA sub-phase cannot be signed off while a task is still outstanding. Every task must be either done or deliberately excluded.
Excluding needs a reasonA task that does not apply, because materials never arrived or an inspection is still pending, can be marked not applicable. A reason has to be typed in.
Signing locks itOnce signed, the task list becomes read-only. Correcting something means formally reopening the sub-phase, and who reopened it and when is recorded.
For the pilot

Field crews will not be signing off directly to begin with. Cody and Emon sign off on the crew's behalf, from the office or from a phone, which is why the sign-offs already in the system carry their names. Handing sign-off to the crew leads themselves is a later step, and needs no change to how any of this works.

Reference

Things worth knowing before they bite

Each of these is a place where the system does something deliberate that can catch people out.

A new pool does nothing until it is made live

Pools arrive as Planned. Nothing dispatches, nobody is emailed, and the pool stays off the calendar until its status is set to Active. A pool nobody made live simply sits there doing nothing.

Two working days is only a placeholder

Every sub-phase is created at two working days. Putting in the real durations is the PM's job, and until that is done the dates are only an estimate.

Changing a duration does not move anything

Editing DurationDays records how long a stage should take. The dates only move once a Reschedule Pool request is raised.

Crew is picked from a list

Crew is a dropdown of short codes. A sub-phase with no crew is not dispatched to anybody and will not appear on anyone's day.

Unassigned still counts

Work sitting on Unassigned is scheduled but has nobody on it. It still shows in the schedule, still appears on the calendar, and still counts against the sign-off figures.

Rescheduling moves everything after it

A reschedule from sub-phase 12 moves 12 and every sub-phase that follows. Lock the ones that cannot move, such as booked inspections and confirmed deliveries, before reflowing.

The overnight jobs run once a night

The calendar, the summary emails and the nightly checks are all built once a night, before the working day. The views on the site are always live, so anything entered during the day shows immediately there. It is the calendar, the emails and the flags that catch up the following morning.

Removing a pool removes its history

Deleting a pool takes its sub-phases and every task with it, including the sign-off record. A job that has merely paused should be set to On Hold instead. When in doubt, ask before deleting.

Removing one is deliberately awkward. On the Remove a Pool form, both Title and ConfirmJobNumber have to be the job number itself, typed twice and matching exactly. Anything else, including a description in the Title, is refused. Like building one, removing a pool runs for several minutes.

One loose end to know about: a deleted pool's entries stay on the calendar until they are cleared by hand. They are leftovers, not live work.

If something looks wrong

Nothing here is beyond fixing, and none of it is worth struggling with alone. A schedule that looks wrong, a pool that will not build, a figure that does not match what is known on site: all of it is much easier to correct early.

The most useful thing to send is the pool's job number and what was expected instead.
